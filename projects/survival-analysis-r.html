Survival Analysis — Patient Outcome Prediction

Evaluating patient survival time distribution variance across demographic groups using R, Kaplan-Meier estimation, and Log-Rank hypothesis testing.

MEASURABLE IMPACT

Statistically Validated Cohort Significance

Predicting Clinical Survival Time Variance

In medical data science, accurately modeling time-to-event outcomes (such as patient survival duration post-treatment) is essential for evaluating therapeutic efficacy. A fundamental challenge is determining whether survival times differ significantly across demographic cohorts (e.g., gender) while accounting for right-censored data points.

Implementation in R

I built a complete biostatistical analysis workflow in R:

  • Normality & Distribution Testing: Performed Z-S normality tests, visual histogram analysis, and Q-Q plots to evaluate distribution skewness and parametric assumptions.
  • Kaplan-Meier Survival Function: Plotted non-parametric Kaplan-Meier survival curves to estimate survival probability over time across cohorts.
  • Log-Rank Hypothesis Testing: Executed Log-Rank non-parametric statistical tests to calculate p-values evaluating whether demographic survival functions differed significantly.

What I found

The Log-Rank test showed a statistically significant difference in survival time between the demographic cohorts compared. Beyond the result itself, the more useful part of the exercise was working through where a parametric approach was justified versus where the non-parametric Kaplan-Meier method was the more honest fit for the data.
